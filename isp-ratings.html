NetPilot ISP Ratings — Methodology

Last updated: November 7, 2026

What the ratings show

For each internet service provider (ISP) and place (country, region or city), NetPilot shows statistics calculated from speed tests run by NetPilot users who share anonymous results: the median download speed, median upload speed and median ping, the typical range (10th to 90th percentile), consistency, and the number of tests used.

Where the data comes from

  • Each shared result contains download, upload, ping, jitter, connection type, Wi-Fi band, app version, platform and the hour of the test. See the Privacy Policy.
  • The ISP and the approximate country, region and city are looked up on our server from the test's internet (IP) address using DB-IP Lite. The IP address is not stored.
  • Users can correct their ISP in the app. Results with impossible values are rejected.

How the numbers are calculated

  • Median: half of the tests were faster and half slower. Medians are less affected by unusual results than averages.
  • Typical range: the 10th to 90th percentile of results.
  • Consistency: the share of tests that reached at least half of the median download speed.
  • Periods: the last 30 days and the last 90 days, recalculated every night (UTC).
  • Minimum tests: a rating is only shown when there are at least 50 tests for a city, 30 for a region and 20 for a country in that period. Otherwise the app says there are not enough tests yet.

Limitations

Results depend on who tested, where and when, on Wi-Fi or mobile signal, on the device, and on the plan each user pays for. A lower median can reflect cheaper plans or weaker Wi-Fi rather than the ISP's network. IP-based location is approximate and can place a test in a nearby city. Ratings are statistics for information only, not an official measurement or endorsement.

Opting out

You can stop sharing results at any time in NetPilot: Settings > "Share anonymous results".
